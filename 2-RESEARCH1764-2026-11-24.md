# Checked boundary case for edge ownership

SanThought research notes, November 24, 2026, 02:03 Colombia time.

This is partial mathematical research, not a solution to P vs NP, not a proof of L-meta-1764 or L-meta-1710, and not an accepted upstream contribution. No credit is claimed.

## A complete local result

`Zero1764.lean` proves the following stronger zero-nesting boundary case. Let G be any finite simple graph and C an exact monotone AND/OR circuit computing its edge function. If no AND gate occurs in the strict cone of another AND gate, then every edge has a pair-sound owning AND gate. Consequently, the number of edges without such an owner is exactly zero. No degree or girth condition is needed.

The file also proves that, without the zero-nesting condition, every edge without a pair-sound owner activates two nested AND gates, with the outer gate pair-sound. This is an existence statement, not an amortized counting bound.

The proof follows a true output backward through OR gates. An input-only witness would make the circuit accept a singleton, which an edge function cannot do. Thus it reaches a true AND gate whose truth implies output truth. Zero nesting makes this AND an owner.

Lean 4.36 checked the complete file successfully against the local PEqualNP definitions. The final audit reports only propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound for both `unclean_count_zero` and `unclean_edge_active_nested`, with no unproved placeholders. Helpers through `Dom` are adapted from upstream L-meta-1600; the final output-descent and boundary-case lemmas are the new preparation recorded here. The archived check log records the final audit.

## Failed shortcuts, explicitly limited

* `Obstruction1764-small.lean`: a checked 5-cycle example has an edge all of whose owners are pair-unsafe. This does not refute the posted target: the graph has degree two and fails its degree hypothesis.
* `Guard1764.lean`: checked truth-value boundary and Boolean guard lemmas. Finding a guard does not give a constant bound on how many edges it must pay for.
* `obstruction1764-petersen.py` and its JSON output: an exhaustive Python check of a Petersen-graph circuit shows that a naive first-guard assignment can have fifteen edges sharing one guard. This diagnostic is not a Lean proof of the full Petersen computation, not a counterexample to the target, and not a lower bound.

## What remains open

The full positive-nesting case needs a justified linear counting argument. The current existence lemmas do not supply it. Existing public reduction PR4115 already splits the target by nested AND-pair count; this record does not duplicate that reduction as a new upstream submission.
