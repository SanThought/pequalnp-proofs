# Petersen graph: a checked 15 non-NOT gate lower bound

SanThought, November 24, 2026.

## Result and limit

Every fan-in-two AND/OR/NOT circuit computing the edge function of the full 10-vertex Petersen graph has at least **15 non-NOT gates**. In particular, a circuit with at most 19 gates has **at most 4 NOT gates**.

This is a checked partial result, **not a proof of L-meta-2592**. That assignment rules out every size-at-most-19 circuit with at least one NOT. The cases with 1, 2, 3 or 4 NOT gates remain unresolved here. Nothing in this notebook claims P != NP or upstream acceptance.

## Argument

The proof adapts the branch-group circuit argument from the accepted L-meta-2165 contribution, whose source credits L-meta-1549. It changes the graph to all 10 Petersen vertices. A finite mask certificate proves that five twin-class removal steps cannot delete the whole graph. The circuit argument therefore forces at least six branch wires, yielding at least 15 non-NOT gates.

The finite certificate caches exact reachable frontiers through depth four. Instead of constructing and deduplicating the larger fifth frontier, it checks directly that no one-step successor of a fourth-frontier mask is the full mask. All finite computations use `decide +kernel`.

Edges: 01, 12, 23, 34, 40, 05, 16, 27, 38, 49, 57, 79, 96, 68, 85.

## Reproduction

Download the accompanying Petersen research ZIP archive from this repository. Use the project's pinned Lean 4.36.0 environment and built PEqualNP definitions. Add the extracted directory to LEAN_PATH, keeping the project's usual dependency paths. Compile these files in order, generating an `.olean` for each:

1. TenPrefix.lean
2. FrontEarly.lean
3. FrontThree.lean
4. FrontFour.lean
5. FrontFiveSmall.lean
6. TenFinish.lean

The final declarations are `PetersenTen2592.petersen_binary_lower` and `PetersenTen2592.at_most_four_NOTs`. Both report only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`. Compilation succeeded locally for every module; final output is included in `finish3.log`. The modular layout avoids expensive repeated elaboration and list deduplication. Earlier timed-out experiments are not included or represented as checked results.
