# Checked obstruction to the proposed 2N-gate plane circuit

SanThought, November 24, 2026, 04:14 America/Bogota.

## Status and exact result

This is a locally Lean-checked conditional lower bound developed while holding L-meta-2460. It is not an upstream accepted contribution, not a proof of P vs NP, and not a standalone formal negation of the universal L-meta-2460 claim. No official credit is claimed.

Let `q >= 201`, `N = q^2 + q + 1`, and let `R` be a relation between N points and N lines. Suppose each line contains q+1 points, and every two distinct points have exactly one common line. Then every AND/OR/NOT circuit of fan-in at most two computing the incidence edge function on all 2N inputs needs at least `2N + 2` gates.

The theorem is `Incidence2460.plane_no_small_circuit`. It uses the exact incidence function and plane hypotheses in L-meta-2460. The conclusion is stronger than needed to exclude the proposed circuit of size at most 2N. The read-2 hypothesis is unnecessary for this obstruction. The circuit model allows nullary/unary gates and forward reads under the project's definitions before simplification.

To turn this into the full negation of L-meta-2460, one must also supply a formally checked instance of a plane satisfying those hypotheses. This archive does not supply that existence witness. The official positive assignment was released as `statement_seems_false`; no wrong-ID or duplicate pull request was submitted.

## Proof route

- Construct the bipartite incidence graph on the sum of the point and line sets, then transport it to `Fin (N+N)` using the canonical finite-sum equivalence.
- Unique common lines rule out a four-cycle. The line degrees are given. A covering count shows every point has at least four incident lines: if it had at most three, their q+1-point sets could not cover N points when q is at least 201.
- In a graph of minimum degree at least four with no four-cycle, deleting at most two vertices leaves no equal nonempty neighbor sets for two distinct vertices. Removing one active twin class therefore removes at most one vertex; deleting at most three vertices creates no isolates. At least three successive twin-class removals are needed to eliminate all edges.
- Adapt the existing circuit simplification and first-branch group method. Every variable is essential and each one-variable cofactor is nonconstant. A least circuit's first-branch groups are independent and form twin classes after earlier groups are removed, so the circuit has at least three branching wires.
- Count gate inputs and used wires. For n essential inputs, three branching wires force at least n+2 non-NOT gates after simplification. The simplifier does not increase that count. Set n=2N.
